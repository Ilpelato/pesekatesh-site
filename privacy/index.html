Privacy Policy — Pesëkatësh

Last updated 26 September 2026 · in effect from 26 September 2026

Pesëkatësh is a card game. This policy says what it keeps about you, why, who else handles it, and what you can ask us to do about it. It is short because the game collects very little.

In short

  • Playing against the computer needs no account, and your games against it never leave your phone. Under 16, the only thing the app sends is a check for app updates. At 16 and over it also asks our server, when it starts, whether online play is switched on, and sends crash reports unless you turn them off.
  • Online play needs an account, and is only for players 16 and over.
  • There are no ads and no analytics. Nothing is sold, and nobody tracks you across other apps or websites through the game.
  • You can delete your account from inside the app at any time.

Who we are

Pesëkatësh is made by Evelina Simoni, in Italy, who decides what the game's data is used for. That makes Evelina Simoni the "controller" in data protection law, and "we" in this policy. Write to peskac.support@gmail.com about anything here, in English or Albanian.

What stays on your phone

  • Your chosen name, language, house rules, and your sound and crash report settings.
  • Your coins, card backs and tables, and your own match record.
  • A saved match, so you can close the app and carry on.
  • Whether online play is open to you. That is the one thing kept from the age question: the date of birth you typed is never stored, on the phone or anywhere else.
  • If you play online, your sign-in, so you stay signed in.

It stays on the phone, and is removed when you delete the app.

Before the age question, and for players under 16

Right after the language, the app asks your date of birth. Until you answer, and afterwards if the answer is under 16, the app sends one kind of request and nothing else: a check for app updates. When the app starts, and when you come back to it after a while, it asks Expo's update service whether a newer version of the game is ready, and downloads it if there is one. That request carries the app's version, a random number Expo creates for this installation of the app, and, like any request over the internet, your phone's IP address.

It is used only to check for and deliver updates: never to contact you, to build a profile of you or to show you ads, and it is not joined to anything else about you. Nothing else is sent: no account is made, and there are no crash reports and no notifications.

What we keep if you play online

Playing online needs an account. We keep:

  • A username, which other players can see and search for. It does not have to be your real name, and we recommend that it is not.
  • A rating, and how many ranked matches you have left early.
  • Your friends list, and invitations you send or receive.
  • The state of matches you are in, so a game survives losing signal.
  • An email address and a password, only if you create an account or save one, so you can sign in on another phone. The password is stored in a form nobody can read, us included.
  • If you sign in with Apple or Google instead, the email address they give us (Apple can give a private relay address in place of your own) and the identifier of your account with them, so the same account opens next time. There is no password. From Apple we ask for nothing else. Google always sends the name and profile picture on your Google account as well; they are kept with the sign-in record, never shown in the game or to other players, and deleted with the account.
  • A notification token, only if you allow notifications, with the language the app is set to, so notifications arrive in it.
  • How your daily table went: your score on the day's shared deal, which your friends can see.
  • A record of each ranked wait: when you joined the queue and how the wait ended, so we can see whether matchmaking works. It is the only record of use we keep.
  • Reports: the reason you pick when you report someone (and the line you write, if you pick "something else"), and reports other players make about you.

We do not collect your real name (apart from the one Google sends, above), the contacts on your phone, your location, advertising identifiers, or analytics about how you use the app beyond the ranked-wait record. Your friends list is made only of players you add inside the game.

Crash reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a report so the problem can be fixed: what went wrong, where in the code, technical details of the phone such as its model, iOS version, memory and screen, and the last few requests the app made to our server (the address, the method and the answer's status only; never what was sent, a search or a name). A crash report carries no username, account, email address or anything else that identifies you or your phone, and Sentry, which receives it, is set not to keep the IP address it came from or any identifier of the phone. Nothing else about how the app runs is sent: no sessions, no timings, no reports of the app freezing.

Crash reports are only sent for players 16 and over, and you can turn them off at any time with Send crash reports in Settings.

Why we use it

The law asks us to say what gives us the right to use each kind of data (its "legal basis").

  • Your account and online play: signing in, matches, ratings, the ladder, friends, invitations, the daily table and notifications. You asked for these by making an account, and we need the data to provide them. That basis is contract.
  • Keeping online play safe and working: the username filter, reports, moderation and blocking, the server's request log, the ranked-wait record and encrypted backups. That basis is our legitimate interests in a fair game without abuse, which are also yours and every other player's.
  • Crash reports: our legitimate interests in finding and fixing what breaks. They identify nobody, and the switch in Settings turns them off.
  • The update check: our legitimate interests in keeping the game working and secure on your phone.
  • The start-up check (16 and over): when the app starts, it asks our server whether online play is switched on and which ways of signing in are offered, even without an account. Our legitimate interests in showing you online play only when it works.
  • When you write to us (for support, with a request, a complaint or an appeal): our legitimate interests in answering you, or contract where it concerns your account; a request about your rights under data protection law we answer because the law says we must.

Who else handles it

  • Supabase runs our database, the accounts and the game server.
  • Sentry receives crash reports.
  • Expo delivers app updates, and passes notifications on to Apple, which delivers them to your iPhone.
  • GitHub runs our weekly backup job: its servers copy the small tables of our database, encrypt the copy with a key kept as a secret in our GitHub account, and store it for 90 days.
  • Google holds our support mailbox: what you write to peskac.support@gmail.com, and our replies, are kept in Gmail.

Supabase, Sentry and Expo work for us ("processors"): they may use the data only to do that job, on our instructions. GitHub runs the backup job and stores the backups under its own terms and privacy statement, and Google keeps the mailbox under its own terms and privacy policy, as it does for any Gmail account.

Apple and Google are involved in signing in only if you choose to sign in with them. There they are not working for us: they confirm who you are, under their own privacy policies, and send us what is described above. Apart from delivering notifications and holding the support mailbox (above), we send them nothing about you or your games, except that when you delete an account made with Sign in with Apple, we ask Apple to end the game's access to it.

Nothing is sold, and nothing is shared for advertising.

Where the data is, and how it is protected

  • Supabase keeps our database in Ireland, in the EU (the eu-west-1 data centre). Supabase is a company in the United States, and its staff there may reach the data; the EU's standard contractual clauses in its data processing agreement cover that.
  • Sentry keeps crash reports in its EU region, in Germany. Sentry is a company in the United States; transfers to it rely on the EU-US and Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework, with the standard contractual clauses in its data processing agreement as a fallback.
  • Expo is a company in the United States, which handles the update checks and the notifications. Transfers to it rely on the EU-US and Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework, with standard contractual clauses as a fallback.
  • GitHub is a company in the United States, and keeps the encrypted backups there; transfers to it rely on GitHub's certification under the EU-US and Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Google keeps the support mailbox on its servers, which may be in the United States or other countries; Google relies on the EU-US and Swiss-US Data Privacy Framework and on standard contractual clauses for such transfers.

How long we keep it

An account and its record stay until you delete the account. A finished online match is kept for the ladder and the record. A report is kept after a person has decided on it, as the record of that decision; if the reported account is removed, the report keeps the username as it was reported and nothing else about that account. The server's request log, which holds no cards, no names and no account ids, is dropped after 30 days. Crash reports are kept by Sentry for 90 days. Encrypted backups are kept by GitHub for 90 days and then discarded; a deleted account leaves them when they expire. What you write to us, and our replies, are kept in the support mailbox while the matter is open, and deleted once we no longer need them to deal with it or to show how it was dealt with. Supabase keeps the short-lived technical logs any web service keeps, which include the IP address of each request, and for each signed-in session it keeps the IP address and the kind of device or browser it was last used from, until the session ends or the account is deleted; we use neither to identify anyone.

Children

Online play is not offered to anyone under 16. Players under 16 have the whole game against the computer, and nothing about them leaves the phone except the update check described above: no account can be made, so there is no username, no friends, no invitations, no ladder and no crash reports. We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 16.

Sixteen is the age of digital consent that the EU's data protection law (GDPR, Article 8) sets unless a country chooses a lower one (Italy, for example, sets 14), and the age in Albania's law.

If a player seems to be under 16, anyone can report them from the ⋯ beside their name, with the reason Seems to be under 16. A person reads every report within 24 hours. If the player does seem to be under 16, their account is deleted, as described under Deleting your account below. A parent or guardian can also write to peskac.support@gmail.com and ask us to delete their child's account; we will, and we do not need the child's date of birth to do it.

Reporting and blocking

Usernames are visible to other players. They are checked against a list of offensive words when they are chosen, and names that pretend to be staff are refused.

You can report or block any player from the ⋯ beside their name: in search, friend requests, your friends list, the ladder, a table lobby, or at the table. Reporting blocks them too. A person reads the report within 24 hours and may close it, replace an offensive username, or remove the account. The rules are in the terms and community rules.

A player you block can no longer add you or invite you to a table, and any invitations between you are withdrawn. They are not told that you blocked them. You can unblock them from Online → Friends.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from Profile → Save account (or Your account, once it is saved) → Delete my account. This removes your username, rating, friends, invitations and notification token immediately and for good. Matches you played remain, because they are also other players' records, but they are no longer linked to you. A report you made stays, so a person can still act on it, but it is no longer linked to you; a report made about you keeps the username as it was reported. The ranked-wait record loses its link to you.

If you are under 18, you can remove your username and account yourself at any time, in the same place. That removes you from what other players can see, although what they have already seen, such as the results of past matches, may stay with them.

Your rights

You can ask us:

  • for a copy of the data we hold about you, including in a form another service can read;
  • to correct it;
  • to delete it (deleting the account in the app is the quickest way);
  • to stop using it where we rely on our legitimate interests, if your situation gives you a reason to object, and to limit what we do with it while a correction or an objection is being looked at.

Write to peskac.support@gmail.com from the email address on the account, or tell us your username; Request my data, beside Delete my account in the app, starts that email for you. We answer within a month.

Privacy complaints

If you think we have handled your data wrongly, tell us first: write to peskac.support@gmail.com with the subject Privacy complaint, or use Privacy complaint beside Delete my account in the app. We acknowledge every complaint within 30 days, look into it, and tell you what we found and what we did about it.

You can also complain to a data protection authority at any time, whether or not you have written to us: in the EU, the one where you live or work, or Italy's Garante per la protezione dei dati personali, the authority where we are; in Albania, the Commissioner for the Right to Information and Personal Data Protection; in Kosovo, the Information and Privacy Agency; in Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. If a change affects what we collect or why, we say so in the app before it takes effect.
