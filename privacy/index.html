Privacy Policy — Pesëkatësh

Last updated: 20 September 2026

Pesëkatësh is a card game. This policy describes what it stores and why. It is short because the app collects very little.

What is stored on your device

  • Your chosen name, language, house-rule settings and sound preference.
  • Your coins, owned card backs and tables, and your own match record.
  • A saved match, so you can close the app and carry on.

This never leaves your phone unless you play online, and it is removed when you delete the app.

What is stored on our server, if you play online

Playing online creates an account. We store:

  • A username, which other players can see and search for.
  • A rating, and how many ranked matches you have left early.
  • Your friends list, and invitations you send or receive.
  • The state of matches you are in, so a game survives losing signal.
  • An email address and a password, only if you create an account or save one, so you can sign in on another phone. The password is stored in a form that nobody can read, including us.
  • A notification token, only if you allow notifications.

We do not collect your real name, the contacts on your phone, your location, advertising identifiers, or any analytics about how you use the app. (Your friends list is kept, as above, but it is made only of players you add inside the game.) There is no advertising, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

Children

Pesëkatësh is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. It contains no mature content; its App Store age rating reflects a daily prize wheel that awards in-game coins at random. Coins cannot be bought or exchanged for anything of real value.

A player is only ever asked for a username, which does not have to be a real name — and we recommend it is not.

Reporting and blocking

Usernames are visible to other players. They are checked against a list of offensive words when they are chosen, and names that pretend to be staff are refused.

You can report or block any player from the ⋯ beside their name — in search, friend requests, your friends list, the ladder, a table lobby, or at the table. Reporting blocks them too. The reason you pick — and, if you pick "something else", the line you write — is stored with the report and read by a person, who may close it, replace an offensive username, or remove the account.

A player you block can no longer add you or invite you to a table, and any invitations between you are withdrawn. They are not told that you blocked them. You can unblock them from Online → Friends.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from Profile → Save account (or Your account, once it is saved) → Delete my account. This removes your username, rating, friends, invitations and notification token immediately and permanently. Matches you played remain, because they are also other players' records, but they are no longer linked to you.

Crash reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a report so the problem can be fixed: what went wrong, where in the code, and the phone model and iOS version. Crash reports do not include your username, account, email address or IP address, and they are not used for anything else.

Where the data lives

Online data is held by Supabase (PostgreSQL) in the EU. Notifications are delivered through Expo's push service. Crash reports are held by Sentry in the EU.
