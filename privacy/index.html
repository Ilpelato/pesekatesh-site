Privacy Policy — Pesëkatësh

Last updated: 25 September 2026

Pesëkatësh is a card game. This policy describes what it stores and why. It is short because the app collects very little.

What is stored on your device

  • Your chosen name, language, house-rule settings and sound preference.
  • Your coins, owned card backs and tables, and your own match record.
  • A saved match, so you can close the app and carry on.
  • Whether online play is open to you: the one thing kept from the age question. The date of birth you typed is not stored, on the phone or anywhere else. If an answer closed online play, the phone also keeps the day it was given, so the question is not asked again for a day.

This never leaves your phone unless you play online, and it is removed when you delete the app.

What is stored on our server, if you play online

Playing online creates an account. We store:

  • A username, which other players can see and search for.
  • A rating, and how many ranked matches you have left early.
  • Your friends list, and invitations you send or receive.
  • The state of matches you are in, so a game survives losing signal.
  • An email address and a password, only if you create an account or save one, so you can sign in on another phone. The password is stored in a form that nobody can read, including us.
  • If you sign in with Apple or Google instead, the email address they give us (Apple can give a private relay address in place of your own) and the identifier for your account with them, so the same account opens next time. There is no password. From Apple we ask for nothing else. Google always sends the name and profile picture on your Google account as well; they are kept with the sign-in record, never shown in the game or to other players, and deleted with the account.
  • A notification token, only if you allow notifications, with the language the app is set to, so that notifications arrive in it.
  • How your daily table went: your score on the day's shared deal, which your friends can see.
  • A record of each ranked wait: when you joined the queue and how the wait ended, so we can see whether matchmaking works. It is the only record of use we keep.

We do not collect your real name (other than the one Google sends, above, if you sign in with Google), the contacts on your phone, your location, advertising identifiers, or any analytics about how you use the app beyond the ranked-wait record above. (Your friends list is kept, as above, but it is made only of players you add inside the game.) There is no advertising, and nothing is sold or shared with anyone.

Children

Before anything else, the app asks every player their date of birth, once. The date is not stored, on the phone or anywhere else; the app keeps only what it decided from it, which is whether online play is open to you. The question is neutral: nothing on the page says what age matters, and an answer that closes online play cannot be changed for a day.

Players under 16 can play the whole game against the bots. Nothing they do leaves the phone: no account can be created, so there is no username, no friends, no invitations, no ladder, and no crash reports are sent. The Online choice in the app says so in place of opening.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone under 16. Sixteen is the age of digital consent in the EU's data protection rules (GDPR, Article 8) where a country has not set a lower one, which is the highest age any EU country sets and above the ages set elsewhere the game is played: Albania's data protection law follows the same rule, and the United States (COPPA) and the United Kingdom set 13. If a younger player has reached online play by giving a wrong date, a parent or guardian can write to the address below and we will delete the account and everything held with it; the player can also delete it themselves from Profile. A mistyped date can be corrected under Settings → Online play → Change, after the day's wait.

The app contains no mature content; its App Store age rating reflects a daily prize wheel that awards in-game coins at random. Coins cannot be bought or exchanged for anything of real value. A player is only ever asked for a username, which does not have to be a real name, and we recommend it is not.

How long we keep it

An account and its record stay until you delete the account. A finished online match is kept for the ladder and the record. A report is kept after a person has decided on it, as the record of that decision; if the reported account is removed, the report keeps the username as it was reported and nothing else about that account. The server's request log, which holds no cards, no names and no account ids, is dropped after 30 days. Crash reports are kept by Sentry for 90 days. Encrypted backups of the small tables are held by GitHub for 90 days and then discarded; only we hold the key, and a deleted account leaves them when they expire. Supabase keeps the short-lived technical logs any web service keeps, which include the IP address of each request; we do not use them to identify anyone.

Reporting and blocking

Usernames are visible to other players. They are checked against a list of offensive words when they are chosen, and names that pretend to be staff are refused.

You can report or block any player from the ⋯ beside their name — in search, friend requests, your friends list, the ladder, a table lobby, or at the table. Reporting blocks them too. The reason you pick — and, if you pick "something else", the line you write — is stored with the report and read by a person, who may close it, replace an offensive username, or remove the account.

A player you block can no longer add you or invite you to a table, and any invitations between you are withdrawn. They are not told that you blocked them. You can unblock them from Online → Friends.

Deleting your account

You can delete your account from Profile → Save account (or Your account, once it is saved) → Delete my account. This removes your username, rating, friends, invitations and notification token immediately and permanently. Matches you played remain, because they are also other players' records, but they are no longer linked to you. A report you made stays, so a person can still act on it, but it is no longer linked to you; a report made about you keeps the username as it was reported. The ranked-wait record loses its link to you.

Crash reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, it sends a report so the problem can be fixed: what went wrong, where in the code, and the phone model and iOS version. Crash reports do not include your username, account, email address or IP address, and they are not used for anything else. Nothing else is sent about how the app runs: no sessions, no timings, no reports of the app freezing. For players under 16 the crash reporter does not run at all.

Where the data lives

Online data is held by Supabase (PostgreSQL) in the EU. Notifications are delivered through Expo's push service. Crash reports are held by Sentry in the EU. Apple and Google are involved only if you choose to sign in with them. They confirm who you are, and Google also sends the name and picture described above. We send them nothing about you or your games; when you delete an account made with Sign in with Apple, we only ask Apple to end the game's access to it.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of the data we hold about you, have it corrected, or have it deleted, by writing to the address below; deleting the account from inside the app is the quickest way. If you think we have handled your data wrongly, you can complain to the data protection authority where you live.
